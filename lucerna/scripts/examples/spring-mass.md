# The ideal spring oscillator

This original teaching example uses a mass m attached to a Hooke-law spring of stiffness k on a frictionless horizontal surface. Displacement x is measured from equilibrium. The force is F=-kx and acceleration is -kx/m.

## Motion and energy

Starting from rest at amplitude A, displacement is x=A cos(sqrt(k/m)t) and velocity is -A sqrt(k/m) sin(sqrt(k/m)t). The angular frequency is sqrt(k/m). Spring potential energy is kx squared divided by two, kinetic energy is mv squared divided by two, and their sum is constant at kA squared divided by two.

## Model boundaries

The model omits friction, air resistance, spring mass and nonlinear spring response. Screen positions magnify the physical displacement and show explanatory geometry rather than a scaled engineering drawing. The animation amplitude is one distance unit.
